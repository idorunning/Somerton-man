# RPFA methodology

RPFA is the repository's working framework for assessing **route-pattern fit and alternatives**. The name and scoring model may be refined as the research develops.

## Purpose

To compare candidate transport interpretations consistently without turning superficial initial matches into conclusions.

## Unit of analysis

A candidate interpretation maps an inscription token or letter to a historically documented station, stop, junction, route or place.

## Proposed scoring dimensions

| Dimension | Question |
|---|---|
| Historical validity | Did the location or service exist at the relevant time? |
| Geographic continuity | Can successive candidates form a coherent route? |
| Timetable feasibility | Could the movements occur within the available time? |
| Pattern economy | How many letters are explained without special pleading? |
| Repetition fit | Are repeated groups explained consistently? |
| Source quality | Are claims supported by contemporary or authoritative records? |
| Alternative density | How many equally plausible matches exist? |

## Procedure

1. Freeze the transcription variant being tested.
2. Generate candidates independently of the desired route.
3. Apply date and geography filters.
4. Score all surviving candidates using declared rules.
5. Compare against null and alternative hypotheses.
6. Run sensitivity checks using plausible transcription variants.
7. Record exclusions and judgement calls in `decisions.md`.

## Guardrails

- Do not choose a station solely because its initial fits.
- Do not use modern station names without historical confirmation.
- Report ambiguity and negative results.
- Keep raw data separate from derived scores.
- Make every result reproducible from versioned inputs.
