# Could the Somerton Man letters record a journey?

## Full review of the itinerary theory, source maps and next investigations

**Prepared for Nathan Tracey · 5 September 2026**  
**Scope:** the existing Somerton Man project, the route and place-name hypothesis, recovered maps, computational claims and opportunities for further investigation. This is a research review, not a finding about criminal responsibility or the cause of death.

## Assessment

**The itinerary theory remains unproved. It deserves a more disciplined test, but the existing matches do not establish a route, its traveller or its purpose.** The strongest opportunity is to investigate whether the ordered letters fit a historically available journey better than equally flexible alternatives. The present evidence cannot support the published account of Webb systematically searching for Jessica Thomson.

Three questions must remain separate: whether the writing contains place initials; whether those places describe a real or intended journey; and whether Webb wrote it while seeking a particular person. Success on the first question would not answer the other two. The writing’s author and date remain unknown.

This review also corrects the project’s own critical work. A map inventory counted incorrectly; several photographs were misidentified; a cancelled line was inconsistently handled; and a limited test of four corridor strings was sometimes described too broadly. Correcting these weaknesses improves the investigation without implying that every travel-note interpretation has been disproved.

The research identification of the deceased as Carl/Charles Webb is highly consequential background, but it is not evidence that he wrote the letters. The latest specific status report inspected here is ABC’s 6 July 2026 article, which records that police described their coronial investigation as ongoing. Abbott’s expectation of an imminent report was not an official finding. No later published determination was verified in this review. [ABC, 6 July 2026](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-07-06/sa-police-report-coroner-into-somerton-man-case-very-soon/106869302)

## 1. What was reviewed

The review examined the recovered project repository, its hypothesis and method documents, prior findings on letter coverage and geographic centres, the two substantive reviews dated 21 August 2026, Nathan’s October and December 2025 articles, and the existing interactive map’s implementation. The university code-analysis reports and relevant historical sources were checked alongside that work.

The map audit inspected all **20 recovered repository JPEGs** in overview and examined important details at the available resolution. It also extracted and inspected the unchanged map image embedded in the existing interactive page. The Google Drive folder contained **19 files**, verified through its metadata. Attempts to retrieve the original image bytes failed with HTTP 403. Consequently, this review does not claim that the 19 Drive originals were visually reinspected.

The two August reports remain useful research records, but their assertions require the same scrutiny as outside sources. Their text was recovered; linked exhibits were not all newly opened. Previously reported calculations are identified as historical results unless reproduced during this review. The surviving source material is enough to audit the theory’s structure, but not to reconstruct the complete transport system operating on 30 November 1948.

## 2. The writing must be fixed before locations are selected

A defensible working transcription preserves four retained lines and the separate cancelled line:

| Physical line | Working transcription | Characters | Treatment |
|---|---|---:|---|
| 1 | `{M/W}RGOABABD` | 9 | Retained; initial reading varies |
| 2 | `MLIAOI` | 6 | Cancelled; retain as a separate observation |
| 3 | `{M/W}TBIMPANETP` | 11 | Retained; initial reading varies |
| 4 | `MLIABOAIAQC` | 11 | Retained; final C/S proposal requires separate examination |
| 5 | `ITTMTSAMSTGAB` | 13 | Retained |

These readings give **44 retained characters, or 50 including the cancelled six**. An additional crossing mark should not silently become a letter. This table is an analytical convention, not a new forensic determination of the handwriting.

The October 2025 article printed a terminal D where the later version uses P, and marked the long `MLIABOAIAQC` line as cancelled. The recovered repository repeated the wrong cancellation and accused imported RPFA work of mistakenly excluding the short line. That criticism was reversed: excluding the genuinely cancelled short line from an analysis of retained text is reasonable. Analyses using different inputs cannot be pooled as if they tested the same object. [Nathan Tracey, October 2025](https://medium.com/@ntracey/decoding-the-somerton-man-how-frequency-analysis-and-railway-maps-may-have-cracked-an-almost-80-03e730bd0c5a)

The coordinating review downloaded and visually inspected the 1,802 × 1,440-pixel published police reproduction. The short line is visibly struck through; separate horizontal marks above the long line should not be mistaken for cancellation of that line. This is inspection of a reproduction, not examination of the original book. [Published police image](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:SomertonManCode.jpg)

The cancelled `MLIAOI` shares its first four letters with the later retained `MLIAB…` line. A restart or correction is therefore plausible. It could occur in a journey plan, remembered prose, poetry, puzzle working or an encoded message. The December article’s assertion that a cipher would not be crossed out cannot distinguish those alternatives. [Nathan Tracey, December 2025](https://medium.com/@ntracey/the-somerton-man-code-finally-solved-3b0711ee5109)

Two independent readers should record each uncertain glyph before seeing proposed expansions. The project should retain the image, each reading, its reason and the resulting analysis version. M/W and C/S changes must be tested openly; selecting S because it creates AQS would reverse the proper direction of evidence.

The earlier forensic-analysis PDF also needs correction. Its numerical handwriting measurements and claims about pressure, stroke characteristics or authorship lack a demonstrated measurement method and authenticated comparison material. Letter shapes in the available reproduction cannot identify Carl Webb, establish his mental state or reveal motive. Those assertions should be withdrawn unless supported by reproducible examination. The route hypothesis should stand on its transport and documentary evidence.

## 3. What the maps establish

The recovered image set comprises **19 photographs of the South Australian railway map and one separately framed national railway map**. The national image is not visibly an inset on the same sheet, and its publication date has not been verified. The previous grouping of 14 + 3 + 2 + 1 images totals 20 and cannot describe a 19-file Drive folder.

The South Australian title block identifies a map accompanying the Railways Commissioner’s report for the year ended **30 June 1938**, with Parliamentary Paper No. 47. The schedule carries the same date. This establishes the map’s documentary context. It does not prove the status of every passenger service ten years later.

Twelve South Australian camera filenames recur in the Drive listing. Their repository copies are approximately 95–192 kB, while same-named Drive files are about 2–3 MB. Matching names do not prove identical bytes; the repository images should be described as derivatives, not untouched originals.

The metropolitan enlargement visibly names relevant railway locations, including Marino, Marino Rocks, Henley Beach, Woodville, Cheltenham and Port Adelaide. Queenstown is not labelled as a stopping place in that inset. It is visible on the separate coloured *Adelaide Environs* image. This establishes a place-name on that image, not a railway station or 1948 passenger service. These findings come from the project’s independent map audit and file inventories dated 5 September 2026.

The environs image has a title, scale bars and printed page number 136, but no verified publication date or catalogue identity. It should remain an undated source until identified. The railway enlargement also uses a different scale from the surrounding state sheet, so the two cannot be calibrated as one continuous geographic surface.

## 4. The current overlay creates false precision

The existing map places manually specified coordinates over a skewed photograph using a generic map projection. The recovered implementation contains no surveyed control-point pairs, transformation record, residual errors or validation. Its lines therefore cannot establish street-level positioning, historical journey distances or travel times.

The repair should provide two complementary views: a geographic map with explicitly approximate historical locations, and a zoomable source-image viewer. The original image must remain available. Any later rectified version should record its transformation and accuracy checks; the metropolitan inset needs separate calibration.

A line drawn between two plausible places is only a proposed connection until a period source establishes the service. An animated route is particularly persuasive, so animation should follow the evidence labels rather than imply that the deceased travelled along a reconstructed path. The unknown last movement to the beach should remain visibly unknown.

Earlier geographical assertions must also come out of the factual layer: that Marion station opened in 1954, that Cheltenham was immediately beside Glenelg, and that the named routes had the published short leg distances. These were not independently established by this review. The December article's 52 Moseley Street address also conflicts with the 90A address used in the later project research. Correcting the source register and attaching the relevant address record must precede use of either as a precise route anchor. [December article](https://medium.com/@ntracey/the-somerton-man-code-finally-solved-3b0711ee5109)

## 5. What the travel evidence actually constrains

The historical evidence supports travel through central Adelaide and a ticket associated with a service towards the coastal district. It does not supply a complete itinerary.

| Item | Supported boundary | What remains unknown |
|---|---|---|
| Used bus ticket `CB88708` | 7d fare; 11:15 St Leonard’s run; issued in the city zone between the railway station and West/South Terrace | Passenger identification and alighting stop |
| Unused Henley Beach railway ticket | First of three sold during the clerk’s roughly 06:15–14:00 shift | Purchase time and intended train |
| Suitcase docket `G52703` | Stamp “11” denotes 11:00–noon | Depositor’s identity and exact time |
| Melbourne arrival | A 09:15 train was an available arrival | Whether the deceased used it |

The transcript gives competing 10:45/10:50 references for the proposed Henley train. Neither fixes a departure intended by the ticket holder. These boundaries derive from the transport witnesses and subsequent reconstruction in the [1949 inquest transcription](https://www.eleceng.adelaide.edu.au/personal/dabbott/wiki/index.php/The_Taman_Shud_Case_Coronial_Inquest).

The book reached police on 22 July 1949, after the June inquest. The code and traced telephone number therefore belong to a later stage of the inquiry; the first inquest does not establish their author or date. [State Records of South Australia](https://archives.sa.gov.au/finding-information/discover-our-collection/stories-from-the-archive/but-what-poison-mystery-of-the-somerton-man)

The prior full review also records public advertisements connecting X3239 with the Thomson household and commercial activity. That weakens an inference from the number alone to an intimate relationship with Jessica. The original advertisement trail remains a priority for page-level checking and source attachment, including the [25 September 1948 notice](https://trove.nla.gov.au/newspaper/article/43784557). A witnessed meeting, prior correspondence or independent record of contact would carry substantially more weight than a route fitted around the household.

## 6. Which analyses survive review?

### Place-name coverage is insufficient

The repository recognises that its candidate pool supplies a place for every code letter. Once that condition holds, rearranging those same letters leaves coverage unchanged. This is a mathematical property of the test: it cannot distinguish the observed order from any permutation with the same letters.

This does not mean that every possible alphabet would pass, or that geography is useless. It means that the completed coverage exercise cannot demonstrate an itinerary. A smallest covering circle has the same limitation: it describes a set of candidate names, not a sequence of journeys.

The repository reports a 14.39 km covering radius from the Thomson address, ranked 111th among 161 centres, against a minimum of 5.40 km near West Croydon. These are prior results, not newly reproduced findings. Their candidate pool, transcription and population thresholds need recovery before reuse. Neither a favourable nor unfavourable circle identifies someone’s lodging.

### The four-corridor test has a narrow negative result

The coordinating review reran exact string comparisons against the four frozen corridor sequences recorded in the August work:

| Corridor | Recorded station-initial string | Longest contiguous overlap |
|---|---|---:|
| Adelaide–Marino Rocks | `AMKGEEWAOHBSSMM` | 2 |
| Adelaide–Henley Beach | `ANBCWKWWASGGKMH` | 2 |
| Adelaide–Port Adelaide | `ANBCWKWWHCAP` | 2 |
| Serviceton–Tailem Bend | `SWBCWBKBCKTCCKYCCT` | 1 |

No complete retained code line matches these strings in either direction under the **eight variants** combining independent M/W choices at the starts of physical lines 1 and 3 with C/S at the end of physical line 4. The rerun comprised 256 line–corridor–direction–variant comparisons. The Serviceton maximum is **one**, correcting the earlier account. This verifies a bounded computational result against four supplied strings. It is not a fresh validation of every source-map transcription, a test of the entire 1948 railway network, or a multimodal reconstruction.

The strict hypothesis that a retained line records every consecutive station on one of those corridors fails this test. Models involving selected destinations, transfers, buses, streets or actions remain untested. Giving those models additional freedom also requires stronger controls.

### The frequency figures cannot establish significance

The October article reports χ² values of 103.36 for ordinary English and 66.57 for station initials, but the complete inputs and calculation are absent. A lower score is not automatically a statistically significant preference, and ordinary English letters are an inadequate sole comparator for a proposed initialism. Word initials differ from letters throughout words. [October analysis](https://medium.com/@ntracey/decoding-the-somerton-man-how-frequency-analysis-and-railway-maps-may-have-cracked-an-almost-80-03e730bd0c5a)

With 44 observations distributed across 26 letter categories, average expected count is only about 1.69. Standard χ² approximations need sufficient expected frequencies; a declared simulation or exact procedure is preferable here. Moreover, real journeys revisit hubs, so uniform sampling of distinct station names is not automatically a realistic travel model. [NIST statistical guidance](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/software/dataplot/refman1/auxillar/chsqgood.htm)

## 7. What previous code research contributes

The Adelaide reports are original student project work, not peer-reviewed demonstrations of a solution. The 2013 analysis conditionally favours English or closely related Scots if the letters are word initials. The 2015 report describes zero-frequency adjustments and unusable significance calculations. The 2016 work includes cancelled letters to reach 50 and acknowledges weak short-sample discrimination. None compares a complete dated transport model against a complete prose-initial model. [2013 report](https://www.eleceng.adelaide.edu.au/personal/dabbott/wiki/index.php/Semester_B_Final_Report_2013_-_Cipher_cracking), [2015 report](https://www.eleceng.adelaide.edu.au/personal/dabbott/wiki/index.php/Final_Report/Thesis_2015), [2016 report](https://www.eleceng.adelaide.edu.au/personal/dabbott/wiki/index.php/Final_Report/Thesis_2016)

John Rehling’s analysis usefully distinguishes finding a matching pre-existing text from composing words to fit the initials. His corpus search did not recover the full text, but that cannot exclude unsearched publications or private writing. Initialisms admit numerous plausible expansions. Place-name selection has the same problem when locations may be chosen freely. [Rehling, March 2014](https://nlpconfidential.blogspot.com/2014/03/murder-and-nlp-taman-shud-case-part-2.html)

Alternative classes should therefore remain visible: remembered prose or poetry; original private notes; betting or puzzle material; travel plans; and writing by another person. Their inclusion is a requirement for comparison, not evidence that any one is correct. Ruth Balint’s historical study documents how incompatible confident readings accumulated around the case. [Balint, 2010](https://epress.lib.uts.edu.au/journals/index.php/csrj/article/download/1520/1823)

## 8. Queenstown and the AQS opportunity

Queenstown is a legitimate historical locality lead. However, using an old tramway to connect it to Port Adelaide creates a dating problem. The Tramway Museum records closure of the Albert Park tram line in November 1934 and the remaining Port Adelaide lines in July 1935. Their earlier existence cannot establish a 1948 tram journey. A bus or other connection requires its own dated evidence. [Australian Electric Transport Museum](https://www.trammuseumadelaide.com/port-adelaide)

The proposed S reading also deserves a more precise assessment. The transport research located **“Adelaide Quarantine Station” in 1887**, in correspondence reproduced in a report about the *Preussen*. This prevents dismissal of the phrase as wholly invented. It does not establish the abbreviation AQS, usage in 1948 or a connection to Webb. The original page was inspected by the transport researcher; a second retrieval failed. [Sydney Morning Herald, 6 January 1887](https://trove.nla.gov.au/newspaper/article/28353190)

The National Archives identifies the institution as Torrens Island Quarantine Station and states that access was by launch or barge until a bridge was built in 1962. A proposed 1948 visit therefore needs a water leg. Relevant catalogued records include the visitors’ book, hospital admissions and nurses’ report book spanning 1948. Their contents were **not inspected** here. [National Archives](https://www.naa.gov.au/help-your-research/fact-sheets/torrens-island-quarantine-station-south-australia)

Interpreting three letters as one facility also changes the encoding rule. It should be tested as a separate model alongside one-letter-per-place readings. The useful next question is whether an independent record connects Webb, a relevant associate or a documented journey with the station. Further ingenious expansion of the letters would add much less.

## 9. Ranked priorities for the next investigation

1. **Lock the transcription and grammar.** Independently code uncertain glyphs; preserve both versions. Specify whether tokens mean stations, destinations, actions or multi-letter abbreviations. Define consistent treatment of repeated letters and cancelled material.

2. **Build the actual 1948 transport network.** Prioritise MTT working timetables, fare stages, the St Leonard’s running journal, SAR notices and contemporary directories. Record mode, service date, stop order, interchange and source for each connection. Distinguish infrastructure from scheduled passenger service.

3. **Recover the original candidate data and calculations.** Version the gazetteer, name variants, geographic boundary and all exclusions. Reproduce the published frequency and radius results before giving them further evidential weight. Preserve failures and revisions.

4. **Run a preregistered comparison.** Fit the same route algorithm to the code, shuffled controls preserving line lengths and letter counts, and period prose/poetry initials. Optimise every control with the same freedom as the real text. Fix scoring, time budgets and transfer penalties beforehand; report all attempted variants. Flexible analysis otherwise inflates apparently favourable results. [Simmons, Nelson and Simonsohn, 2011](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797611417632)

5. **Seek an independent prediction.** A newly recovered appointment, address, handwriting specimen or dated note could test the interpretation. Existing Adelaide/Glenelg clues already used to construct it cannot serve as independent confirmation. Any statistical result should remain conditional on its model.

6. **Investigate contact and quarantine through records.** Follow the publicly used telephone number, household occupancy and relevant advertisements. Request specific 1948 entries in the catalogued quarantine records. Record access failures separately from searches that found no match.

## 10. Refreshing the website responsibly

The redesigned site should lead with **“Could the letters record a journey?”**, followed immediately by the current unproved assessment. Its central view should combine a generous map, a readable transcription panel and a short evidence timeline. Readers should be able to switch between documented ticket evidence, historical infrastructure and candidate interpretations without losing their place.

Every proposed leg needs its mode, date support, source and uncertainty. Source images should open beside the relevant claim, with clear captions explaining document date and inspection limits. A correction register should expose changes to transcription, map identification and previous calculations. The old articles should remain traceable as earlier interpretations with dated correction notes.

Cream backgrounds, dark readable text and restrained green, blue and amber can provide an archival character while maintaining contrast. Colour must be accompanied by words and line patterns. Avoid invented travel-time totals, dramatic verdicts and animations suggesting observed movement. The design should make evidence easier to inspect.

The full deployed site source could not be retrieved through the available browser route because that action was blocked by browser policy. This report supplies an integration-ready research and design specification; it does **not** claim that the live website has been refreshed or redeployed. The remaining work is to integrate the corrected material into an accessible source checkout, verify the resulting page and confirm the deployment.

The most valuable development is now clear: replace successful name-finding with a reproducible test of sequence, historical feasibility and independent prediction. Until that is done, the route theory should remain an interesting working explanation with explicit evidential limits.
