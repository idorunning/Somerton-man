**Date:** prior session, undated
**Verdict: 90A Moseley Street is a poor geometric centre for the code, ranking 111th of 161
candidate centres. This contextualises the hypothesis. It neither supports nor falsifies it.**

## Reading the result

To cover every letter of the code from Moseley Street, the lens has to open to 14.39 km, and
what forces it that wide is Islington in the inner north — the letter I's nearest residential
candidate from a Glenelg anchor.

The tightest lens available anywhere in the metropolitan area is 5.40 km, in the inner
north-west. That is under 40% of the radius Moseley Street requires. The code's letter set has
a natural geographic centre and Glenelg is not it.

## What this does and does not mean

It does not falsify anything. A man travelling *out* from Glenelg to search would generate
exactly this pattern — a home base at the edge of the area covered, with the destinations
clustered elsewhere. Eccentricity of the anchor is compatible with the hypothesis and is
arguably predicted by it.

What it removes is an unstated argument. Under the earlier transport-stop frame, the Glenelg
corridor's network density made Moseley Street read as reasonably central, and that apparent
centrality was doing quiet supporting work. Under a pool built from where people actually
lived, it disappears. The anchor sits in the bottom third.

Anyone arguing from geographic fit now has to argue for a 5.40 km centre near West Croydon,
for which there is no independent evidence at all, or accept that geographic fit is not an
argument for Moseley Street.

## Caveats

- 1947 census data used for 1948 conditions.
- Population thresholds of ≥500 and ≥1,000 are unprincipled round numbers; threshold
  stability of these rankings is untested. See `OPEN_QUESTIONS.md` item 5.
- RPFA is a great-circle geometric measure. It ignores whether any of these places were
  connected to each other or reachable in sequence.
